### 1.3a — RECOMMENDED THRESHOLD GATE SEQUENCE (T0-RT-009)

The five threshold events that close Type 0 have a recommended sequence. Out-of-sequence achievement generates corresponding Axis D debt entries.

**Recommended: Epistemic → Sovereignty → Energy → Space → Sealing**

- Epistemic before Sovereignty: you cannot govern what you cannot verify
- Sovereignty before Energy: planetary energy transition requires binding authority to enforce
- Energy before Space: a civilization that expands off-world while fossil-fuel dependent exports the dependency
- Space before Sealing: technically optional, but ungoverned off-world territories (Space Gate without Sovereignty Gate) generate significant Axis D debt
- Sealing always last: the record is only meaningful if what is being sealed is already verified and governed

This sequence applies as the primary specification for Type 0. Later tiers have their own gate structures specced in their tier documents.

**Gate sequence type varies by tier (IO-RT-013):**
- **Advancement tiers** (Type 0, Type I, Type II, etc.) use **recommended sequential** gate sequences — the forcing functions and dependencies create a logical order that minimizes Axis D debt.
- **Consolidation tiers** (Type I.Ω and any future consolidation thresholds) use **parallel** gate structures — there is no crisis-driven sequence in a consolidation tier; all gates can be achieved in any order and must all be complete before the next tier opens.
This distinction is declared in each tier document. The framework does not assume sequential gates for all tiers.

### 1.4 — REGRESSION AND DEBT-LOCK (RT-009)

Regression: Yes — any axis can decline. Bronze Age Collapse (~1200 BCE): multiple civilizations regressed from ~0.5 to ~0.2–0.3. Rome: ~0.65 peak, ~0.35 collapse over two centuries. Simulation renders regression as backward movement on the axis bar.

Debt-Lock: When CDS exceeds 0.90, all tier transition gates freeze regardless of other axis states. Unlocks when:
1. CDS drops below 0.85 AND
2. CDS declining for ≥ 5 years AND
3. At least 2 of 5 CDS components show active remediation

**0.90 threshold rationale (T0-RT-007):** 0.90 is the point where all five CDS components would need to average ≥ 0.90 — meaning every component is simultaneously in critical-failure range. Below 0.90, at least one component has headroom for targeted intervention. Above 0.90, no component has meaningful recovery headroom without civilizational-scale forcing. Individual and institutional action become structurally insufficient above this threshold. [R] — FCL calibration required. The threshold is structurally derived, not arbitrary, but the exact value may shift with calibration data.

Recovery mechanism for Axis D (RT-018):
- Individual: FSVE certification of outputs (↓ UC) · STP sealing of decisions (↓ UD) · Active bias naming (↓ UB)
- Institutional: Mandatory certification laws (↓ UC — S-axis dependent) · Planetary archive (↓ UM — M-axis dependent) · AI audit infrastructure (↓ UC/UB — A-axis dependent)
- Civilizational: Only achievable when Sovereignty reaches Type I threshold. Axis D reduction at civilizational scale is a dependent variable of S, A, and M axes. Individual action is necessary but insufficient. The framework is honest about this.

**COMPLACENCY DEBT — CDS PATHWAY (TI-RT-014):**
Complacency debt is the primary Axis D generation risk for civilizations that have achieved Type I. It accumulates in the absence of crisis, not in the presence of one — making it invisible until it is structural. It manifests through two specific CDS components:
- **UC (Uncertified Circulation):** As re-certification frequency drops, more AI outputs operate on expired certifications. The outputs haven't changed — but the certification that declared them within parameters has expired. UC rises silently.
- **UD (Unsealed Decisions):** As urgency fades, the discipline of sealing major decisions weakens. Decisions that would have been sealed during the crisis period are made informally, undocumented, unsealed. UD rises silently.

Both components are detectable before complacency becomes structural — if the monitoring architecture is operational. When UC and UD begin rising during a non-crisis period, it is a leading indicator, not yet a catastrophe. The FAILURE ATLAS Floor 5 and Floor 6 entries all begin with UC and UD rising silently in the absence of crisis. The instruments detect this. The question is whether anyone is reading them.

**"Working standard" defined (T0-RT-012):** A working alignment standard is one that: (1) produces verifiable predictions about system behavior across defined domains, (2) can be re-certified after system updates without requiring full rebuild, and (3) fails explicitly when the system exceeds its certified parameters — rather than silently. Not perfect alignment. Falsifiable alignment. An alignment standard that cannot be falsified is not a standard — it is a claim.

2. On a maximum interval of 25 years regardless of updates [R — FCL calibration required]
3. When A-I-a capability crosses a new defined threshold
4. When behavioral drift index from continuous monitoring exceeds defined threshold

All four conditions apply independently. The "alignment re-certification status" live node tracks: (a) time since last re-certification, (b) major updates since last re-certification, (c) behavioral drift index. APPROACHING RE-CERTIFICATION state activates when any threshold is nearing.

3. **Public sealing of compliance:** Re-certification results are publicly sealed and accessible to any citizen anywhere in the civilization.
4. **Mandatory institutional review:** The re-certification body itself is subject to sunset clause and must be re-ratified every 100 years. If the civilization has forgotten why re-certification matters, it will fail re-ratification — and that failure is visible in the public archive.

### 1.6a — FOUR CONSTITUTIONAL URGENCY MECHANISMS (IO-RT-005)

**The problem:** Every tier beyond Type 0 is built under decreasing existential pressure. The S-I-M circularity was broken by crisis forcing. But crisis forcing disappears as the civilization advances. How does a civilization maintain its standards when nothing is forcing it to?

**The answer:** Constitutional urgency — built into the governance architecture before urgency fades. These four mechanisms create institutional forcing in the absence of external forcing. They must be specified and ratified at Type I before the Great Shabbat begins.

| Mechanism | How It Works | What It Prevents |
|-----------|-------------|-----------------|
| Sunset clauses | Major governance agreements have expiration dates — must be actively re-ratified, not maintained by inertia | Silent lapse of critical agreements without anyone noticing |

**THE NO-SELF-CERTIFICATION PRINCIPLE (TII-P-004):** A bounded autonomous AI system that has drifted outside certified parameters will certify itself as within parameters.
