### B — SUBSTRATE
**Status:** The civilization is no longer 100% biological — but it is not post-biological either. Type I is the first tier where substrate diversity becomes legally recognized. Lifespan reliably extended beyond 150 years. Neural-digital integration is standard — not universal, but the legal and technical infrastructure exists for any citizen who chooses it. The first synthetic substrate citizens have been recognized under the planetary constitutional framework (enabled by S.U reaching ≥ 0.80).

**The lifespan extension significance — double-edged (TI-RT-008):**

Extended lifespan (~150+ years) is not unambiguously beneficial. The framework must hold both sides.

**Benefits for the M-axis:** The same individual who made a major decision at age 40 may still be alive at age 150 to see its consequences and seal the retrospective record. Institutional knowledge does not walk out the door at retirement. The 40-year expertise gap of Type 0 becomes an 80-year expertise continuity.

**Costs and complications:**
- **Power entrenchment (S-axis drag):** Decision-makers who live 150 years accumulate power, networks, and institutional authority for far longer than in Type 0. Generational turnover — historically a primary mechanism for political renewal — slows dramatically. The S-axis advancement that required new generations bringing new perspectives is harder when the same individuals hold power for a century.
- **Wealth concentration (D-axis increase):** Accumulated wealth over 150-year lifespans creates structural inequality that compounds faster than at 75-year lifespans. The US component of CDS (structural inheritance) rises, not falls, with lifespan extension unless constitutional redistribution architecture is built explicitly.
- **Cultural rigidity:** Fewer generational transitions mean slower cultural evolution. Ideas that were revolutionary at age 40 can become orthodoxy by age 120 — enforced by the same person who generated them.
- **Axis D complication:** The bias encoded into decisions made at age 40 by a 150-year-old decision-maker persists far longer than in Type 0. The individual who made a biased decision in 2120 is still in power in 2180. Remediation requires confronting living decision-makers, not historical records.

**Net assessment [R]:** Lifespan extension is a net positive for M-axis advancement and a potential drag on S-axis and Axis D reduction. The B-axis sub-tier progression must include constitutional redistribution and power-term limits as requirements — not optional features. A civilization that extends lifespan without building structural protections against power entrenchment generates more Axis D debt from lifespan extension than it recovers from improved institutional memory.

**Sub-tiers within Type I:**
- 1.0 entry: Lifespan reliably extended beyond 120 years demonstrated at scale. Neural-digital integration normalized for those who choose it. First synthetic substrate citizenship legal debates concluded — first legal framework in place.
- 1.0 mid: Lifespan reliably extended beyond 150 years. Substrate choice is a constitutional right under planetary framework. First synthetic substrate citizens granted full legal personhood.
- 1.0 complete: No substrate-based discrimination exists in law or practice. The question is no longer "are you biological?" but "are you a citizen of the planetary civilization?" Multiple substrate types coexist with equal legal standing.

**Dependency note:** B cannot reach Type I threshold without S ≥ 0.80 and A ≥ 0.80 — synthetic citizens require the constitutional architecture (S) and the AI alignment certification (A) to be in place first. B is the last of the core axes to advance to threshold at Type I, by design.

**Threshold to reach Type I.Ω:** Substrate diversity maintained and protected across the Great Shabbat millennium. No substrate-based rights regression.
